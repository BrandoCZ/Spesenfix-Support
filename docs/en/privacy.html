In short

Reisekosten has no account, no advertising, no tracking, no analytics and no third-party SDKs. The app sends nothing to the developer or to third parties. Your data stays on your device and, if you are signed in to iCloud, in your own iCloud account.

2. What the app stores

  • Profile: first and last name, company, e-mail address and recipient address for reports, all entered by you. They appear in the PDF header and in the mail.
  • Cost centers and supervisors you create.
  • Reports: trip, meals, nights, mileage, receipts and hospitality details. For hospitality receipts this includes guests (name, company, optional e-mail). Guests are third parties; the app uses these details only for the evidence page of the PDF, and they are deleted with the report.
  • Attachments and scans: photos, scanned receipts and PDFs you add. They are printed into the PDF.
  • Statistics records: when a report is deleted after the retention period and “Keep statistics” is on, an anonymous record remains (dates, cost center, supervisor, amounts; no title, receipts or files). You can delete it in Settings.
  • Settings, such as the retention period (1 or 3 years).

3. Text recognition

Reading amount, date, merchant and receipt type from receipts happens on your device (Apple Vision). Receipts are not sent anywhere for this.

4. Permissions

  • Camera: to scan receipts (iPhone).
  • Photos and files: only through the system pickers; the app sees only what you choose.
  • Notifications: for the open-reports badge, reminders and the notice before old reports are deleted. They are generated locally on the device and contain counts only, no names or amounts. The app asks once.

5. Where data goes

  • Mail: you send the PDF yourself with the system mail sheet (iPhone) or your mail app (Mac) to the address set in the app. The app never sends mail by itself. Your mail provider and the recipient are responsible for transmission and handling on their side.
  • Share and export: PDF, ZIP and CSV files go where you save or share them.
  • iCloud: reports, receipts, attachments, cost centers and statistics records sync between your devices through your private CloudKit database; the profile and settings sync through the iCloud key-value store. The data is stored in your iCloud account at Apple; the developer has no access. Deleting a report syncs as a deletion to all your devices. Without iCloud sign-in, data stays on the device. Apple processes it under its own privacy terms.
  • Diagnostic log: the app keeps a small text log on the device (about 1 MB at most, newest entries) with actions, counts, receipt type names, error codes and the app and system version. It never records names, merchants, amounts, destinations, e-mail addresses, file names or document content. It leaves the device only if you export it in Settings > Diagnostics and pass it on. “Clear Log” deletes it. It is not synced through iCloud.

6. Retention and deletion

You set the retention period (1 or 3 years, default 3). Paid reports count from the submission date, drafts from creation; submitted but unpaid reports are never deleted automatically. At least 30 days before deletion the app shows a notice with an export option. You can delete reports yourself at any time. Removing the app removes its local data; data in iCloud is managed in your device’s iCloud settings.

7. Apple (App Store, TestFlight)

When you download the app through TestFlight or the App Store, and for feedback or crash reports you share via TestFlight or iOS, Apple processes data under its own responsibility. If you agreed to share with app developers, the developer receives crash reports and usage statistics from Apple with technical details such as device model and iOS version, without name or contact details. TestFlight feedback can include your e-mail address and any screenshots you attach. I use it only to fix bugs and improve the app (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

8. This website and GitHub

These pages are hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub Inc., Microsoft). When you visit, GitHub processes technically necessary data such as your IP address in server logs; the developer has no influence on this. The legal basis is the legitimate interest in providing the pages securely (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). GitHub also processes data in the USA, under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework adequacy decision (Art. 45 GDPR). More: GitHub General Privacy Statement.

If you e-mail me, I use your address and message only to reply and delete them when the matter is settled (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). E-mail is received and stored through Apple iCloud Mail; Apple is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection (Art. 21 GDPR). Since the app does not transmit data to the developer, there is usually nothing to delete on my side; write to the address above with any question. You can also complain to a supervisory authority, for example the Berlin Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information.
